# Five Postiz-style apps indiehackers already pay for

Solo founders at $0 to $5k MRR pay for a near-identical core stack (hosting, database, payments, transactional email, an AI coding tool) and then quietly refuse to pay for a second ring of tools that incumbents price at $20 to $90 a month: feedback boards, changelogs, testimonial walls, status pages, affiliate programs and link managers. That second ring is where the open-source landscape is thinnest, and it is the shape Postiz exploited to go from a solo side project to **$260,759 MRR on 7,622 subscriptions** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/postiz)). The five apps below are, in rank order, a feedback board plus changelog (displacing Canny at $79/mo and Headway at $29/mo), a testimonial wall (Senja at $29/mo, a category with literally no open-source project above 16 stars), a hosted status page and uptime monitor (Uptime Kuma has 92k stars and no cloud; OpenStatus starts at $30/mo), an affiliate and referral program (Refgrow at $29/mo and Dub Partners at $90/mo plus 5% of payouts), and a Cloudflare-native link shortener (Dub's public entry tier is now $90/mo, Bitly's is annual-only). Every one runs on Workers, D1 and R2 with no Postgres, Redis or ClickHouse, which is the single biggest reason founders say they will not self-host the current open-source options, and the first two reuse the omarchyapps.com upvote and newsletter Workers almost unchanged. The rest of this report lays out each app's free and paid scope, then the spend data, pain points and Postiz lessons that justify the picks.

Ranking logic: rank 1 has the largest incumbent revenue pool with the weakest open-source field and the most backend reuse; rank 2 is the only category with no open-source competitor at all; rank 3 has the loudest latent demand (the most-starred self-hosted app in the entire map) but a lower price ceiling; rank 4 has the highest willingness to pay per customer but the longest build and a Stripe-integration surface; rank 5 is the fastest to ship but faces an existing Cloudflare-native repo. All five price flat, with no seats, because per-seat pricing is the single most repeated complaint in the pain-point notes and because every indie-scale OSS success in the landscape notes (Rybbit $13, Fider $25, Cap $8.16, Umami $20) prices flat ([Rybbit](https://rybbit.com/pricing); [Fider](https://fider.io/pricing); [Cap](https://cap.so/pricing); [Umami](https://umami.is/pricing)).

### 1. Feedback board, roadmap and changelog: the biggest unclaimed pool

The feedback and changelog category is the largest unclaimed category by incumbent revenue in the whole landscape map: Canny, Featurebase, UserJot, Productboard, Headway and Beamer are all proprietary, and the open-source field is visibly churning. **Canny's Pro tier starts at $79/mo billed yearly** once you exceed 25 tracked users ([Canny](https://canny.io/pricing)); **Featurebase charges $29 per seat** on Growth ([Featurebase](https://www.featurebase.app/pricing)); **Headway Pro is $29/mo** for whitelabel and custom domain ([Headway](https://headwayapp.co/)); **Beamer starts at $59/mo** ([Beamer](https://www.getbeamer.com/pricing)). On the open-source side, **Fider has 4,574 stars** and a $25/mo cloud, **Astuto is archived**, **Feedbase's last push was January 2025**, LogChimp is donation-funded with $62.88 raised, and the two newest entrants, Quackback (312 stars, December 2025) and FeedLog (174 stars, April 2026), have no traction yet ([Fider](https://github.com/getfider/fider); [Astuto](https://github.com/astuto/astuto); [Feedbase](https://github.com/chroxify/feedbase); [LogChimp on Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/logchimp); [Quackback](https://github.com/QuackbackIO/quackback)). Changelog-specific open source is essentially absent: the GitHub changelog topic is dominated by git release-note generators like semantic-release and git-cliff, not product changelog pages ([GitHub topic](https://github.com/topics/changelog)).

The demand signal is indirect but consistent. Changelog and feedback tools never appear as paid lines in the 2025 to 2026 itemised cost posts, even in the $5k MRR projection ([Prompts to Product](https://www.promptstoproduct.com/saas-cost-at-1k-mrr); [socialanimal.dev](https://socialanimal.dev/blog/saas-mvp-cost-2026/)), which the stack notes read as a price-expectation gap rather than lack of need: founders expect $0 to $10 or a one-off for a feedback board, and the incumbents want $29 to $79. The Indie Hackers observation that most free tiers "become too limited once a second person joins the team" applies directly to Canny's 25-tracked-user cap ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/which-saas-do-you-pay-for-as-an-indie-hacker-131cca2b19)).

Free-forever scope: a single Worker plus D1 that any founder deploys with one `wrangler deploy`, giving unlimited boards, posts, upvotes, status labels (planned, in progress, shipped), a public roadmap view, a changelog page and a drop-in embed widget, with one project per install. Paid cloud at **$9/mo flat**: Jessyka hosts it, adds custom domains, changelog email digests to subscribers, private or password-protected boards, unlimited projects, and removes the badge. The $9 point sits below Fider's $25 and under the Headway $29 floor while still being three times Rybbit's price-per-month of revenue per customer, and flat pricing directly answers Featurebase's per-seat model.

Backend reuse is the strongest of the five. The existing `votes` table (app, voter, at) with its `__Host` cookie voter identity and D1 rate limiter in `src/votes.mjs` is a feature-request upvote system already; swapping the `app` column for a post ID and relaxing the allow-list turns it into the board's vote store. The `subscribe.mjs` Resend contacts flow becomes the changelog subscriber list, and a Resend Broadcast per changelog entry is the paid digest feature. Build estimate for a solo developer with AI tooling: five to seven weeks (two for board and votes on the existing code, one for roadmap and statuses, one for changelog and embed widget, one to two for the hosted multi-tenant layer, custom domains via Cloudflare for SaaS, and Stripe billing with the `metadata.app` tag per house rule 6).

### 2. Testimonial wall: the only truly empty category

No open-source testimonial or wall-of-love product with any traction exists, hosted or not. The landscape researcher searched OpenAlternative's Senja and Testimonial.to pages (both empty), GitHub topics `testimonials` and `testimonial` above 100 stars (zero results), repo search above 200 stars (only unrelated hits) and the awesome-selfhosted README (no entry); the sole candidate is **yshmarov/testimonials, a 16-star Rails gem created 2026-07-23** ([OpenAlternative](https://openalternative.co/alternatives/senja); [yshmarov/testimonials](https://github.com/yshmarov/testimonials)). Meanwhile **Senja charges $29/mo** on Starter and $59 on Pro, with the free tier capped at 15 testimonials ([Senja](https://senja.io/pricing)), and **Testimonial.to charges $25/mo** for unlimited text but still only two videos ([Testimonial.to](https://testimonial.to/pricing)). Senja shows up by name in the saasranger solo-founder stack as part of a ~$20 "support" bundle alongside Plain and Notion ([saasranger](https://saasranger.com/blog/indie-hacker-tool-stack-what-successful-solo-founders-actually-use/)), so it is a tool founders know and budget for, just not at $29.

Free-forever scope: a collection form (text, star rating, name, avatar, company), a moderation inbox, a wall-of-love page, and single-card and grid embeds, unlimited text testimonials, one project. Paid cloud at **$7/mo flat**: video testimonials stored in R2 (the storage cost is the real reason incumbents cap video), custom domain, no branding, unlimited projects, and import of X posts as testimonials. That import must read through TwitterAPI.io, never the official X API, per house rule 2, and the user-supplied post text is never rewritten by AI so rule 1's dash sanitiser is not in play. The embed is the growth loop: every wall carries a small "collected with" link, which is how Senja itself grew. Build estimate: three to five weeks, with the form and wall in the first two and R2 video upload, Cloudflare Stream-free transcoding (store the original, play it directly), and hosted billing in the remainder. This is the cheapest app to build on the list and the one with zero open-source competition, which is why it ranks second rather than fourth despite a lower price ceiling than the feedback board. The empty waitlist category (nothing above 276 stars, no OSS + cloud) is a natural later module here because it is the same embed-widget-plus-tiny-table shape and would reuse `subscribe.mjs` directly, but it is left off the shortlist because the notes recorded no incumbent prices to evidence what founders pay for it ([GitHub topic:waitlist](https://github.com/topics/waitlist)).

### 3. Status page and uptime: 92k stars and nobody selling hosting

Uptime Kuma is the most-starred self-hosted product in the whole 30-category map at **92,340 stars**, and it has **no official hosted version**; only third parties like Flaz (from $0.99/mo) and Hosthink resell it ([Uptime Kuma](https://github.com/louislam/uptime-kuma); [Flaz](https://flaz.id/en/cloud-apps/uptime-kuma)). Gatus has 12,286 stars and explicitly "no paid tier" ([Gatus](https://github.com/TwiN/gatus); [Railway template](https://railway.com/deploy/gatus.md)). The only open-source-plus-cloud entrant, OpenStatus, prices Hobby at one monitor and one page and then jumps to **$30/mo** ([OpenStatus](https://openstatus.dev/pricing.md)). The closed incumbents nickel-and-dime exactly the features a solo founder wants: Better Stack includes ten monitors and one page free, then charges **$15/mo per extra status page, $50/mo for a password-protected page and $40/mo per extra 1,000 subscribers** ([Better Stack](https://betterstack.com/pricing)); Instatus Pro is $20/mo for a custom domain ([Instatus](https://instatus.com/pricing)); UptimeRobot restricted its free plan to non-commercial use in December 2024 and charges around $9/mo for Solo ([Notifier guide](https://notifier.so/guides/uptime-kuma-vs-uptimerobot/)). Uptime Robot at $7/mo is one of the few secondary tools that does show up as a paid line in a 2026 indie stack ([Krispy blog](https://blog.krispyai.com/indie-hacker-stack-2026)), so founders demonstrably pay for this category today, just at single-digit prices.

Free-forever scope: a Worker with Cron Triggers and a Durable Object scheduler that runs HTTP, TCP and keyword monitors at one-minute intervals, stores results in D1, renders a public status page with uptime bars and incident posts, and emails the owner on state change. Paid cloud at **$5/mo flat**: hosted, custom domain, status subscribers who get incident emails (the existing Resend subscribe flow, re-keyed per status page), 30-second checks from multiple Cloudflare regions, and Slack or Discord webhooks. The $5 price is deliberately at the "insurance" level the pain-point notes identify as what hosted-OSS buyers actually pay for: "the hosted version takes care of DB migration and backups for them" ([HN, Libredesk thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43158166)). Build estimate: three to four weeks. The risk is the price ceiling, not demand; at $5 you need roughly 1,800 subscribers to match Rybbit's current $9,215 MRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rybbit-analytics)), which argues for shipping this third, after the two higher-ticket apps, and using it as the traffic funnel (status pages are public and link back).

### 4. Affiliate and referral program: the highest ticket, longest build

Below Dub there is nothing. OpenAlternative's entire affiliate and referral category contains exactly one tool, Dub Partners, which **starts at $90/mo with a 5% fee on every payout** ([OpenAlternative category](https://openalternative.co/categories/business-software/marketing-customer-engagement/affiliate-referral-marketing); [Dub pricing](https://dub.co/pricing)). The small open-source entrants are **refref at 234 stars with its last push in March 2026** and Refferq at 131 stars ([refref](https://github.com/amicalhq/refref); [Refferq](https://github.com/Refferq/Refferq)), and awesome-selfhosted has no affiliate or referral entry at all. The closed incumbents are priced for funded teams: **Refgrow at $29, $49 and $99/mo** ([aisotools](https://aisotools.com/pricing/refgrow)), and the Rewardful alternatives list is "mostly commercial" (Tolt, FirstPromoter, PromoteKit) ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/rewardful)). The resentment lever here is the percentage take on top of a subscription, the same pattern founders attack when Kit takes 3.5% plus 30c on digital sales even on its free plan ([TheFrontKit](https://thefrontkit.com/blogs/convertkit-alternatives-2026)) and when Lemon Squeezy and Paddle take 5% plus 50c ([Lemon Squeezy](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing); [Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)).

Free-forever scope: unlimited affiliates and referral links, first-touch or last-touch cookie attribution, a Stripe webhook listener that credits commissions on `checkout.session.completed` and `invoice.paid` and claws back on refund, a commission ledger, and a self-serve affiliate portal. Paid cloud at **$12/mo flat with no payout percentage**: hosted, custom domain on the portal, payout batch export (CSV and PayPal mass-pay format), recurring-commission rules, and basic fraud rules (self-referral, duplicate card fingerprint). Because every project shares the single "Hello Linda" Stripe account, the hosted version must follow house rule 6 to the letter: tag `metadata.app` on everything it creates, verify against its own endpoint secret, and return 200 on foreign events. Customers connect their own Stripe account via a restricted key or Connect, which is the hardest part of the build. Estimate: six to eight weeks, the longest on the list, which is why it ranks fourth despite the best per-customer economics (a $12 flat fee against Dub's $90 plus 5% is the sharpest price contrast of the five).

### 5. Link shortener: fastest to ship, but Sink is already there

Dub, the open-source leader, has moved upmarket: its public pricing page now shows **Business at $90/mo as the entry point** (10k links, 250k events, 5% payout fee) with no free tier listed, and the old link-only Pro at $19 to $24 survives only in help articles ([Dub pricing](https://dub.co/pricing); [Dub help](https://dub.co/help/article/pro-plan)). **Bitly's Core plan is $10/mo billed annually only, Growth is $29/mo**, and the free plan allows five links a month ([Bitly](https://bitly.com/pages/pricing)). The open-source field has a Cloudflare-native project, **Sink at 7,206 stars**, which proves demand for exactly this architecture but is self-deploy only, and Shlink (5,336 stars), whose hosted web client talks to your own server ([Sink](https://github.com/ccbikai/Sink); [Shlink](https://github.com/shlinkio/shlink); [Linux Handbook](https://linuxhandbook.com/blog/shlink-review/)). Link shorteners, like changelogs, never appear as paid lines in the solo-founder cost posts, which is the same price-expectation gap: the market wants $0 to $5, Dub wants $90.

Free-forever scope: unlimited short links, QR codes, click analytics via Workers Analytics Engine or D1, a UTM builder, and one custom domain on the founder's own Cloudflare zone. Paid cloud at **$5/mo flat**: hosted, multiple custom domains, a link-in-bio page, API keys and two-year click retention. Build estimate: two to three weeks, the shortest on the list. It ranks last because the differentiation versus Sink is the hosted tier and a Linux desktop client (a Walker or Vicinae launcher extension that shortens the clipboard URL on a keybind), not the core; that is a real wedge for Omarchy users but a thin one for the general indie market. Ship it when a quick win is needed between the bigger builds, or fold it into app 4 as the attribution layer, which is exactly how Dub justified its own climb to higher prices ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/16/dub-co-is-an-open-source-url-shortener-and-link-attribution-engine-packed-into-one)).

### The Omarchy-first distribution angle shared by all five

Each app ships three ways: `wrangler deploy` to the founder's own Cloudflare account (free forever, no gating, AGPL), a free hosted tier for anyone who installs the companion Linux client through `omarchy pkg add` or the omarchyapps.com listing, and the paid cloud for everyone else. The companion client is small on purpose: a TUI or launcher extension that posts a changelog entry, approves a testimonial, acknowledges an incident, or shortens a link, packaged for the AUR with an Omarchy theme hook the way omasnap, omacut and Aether are, because the Linux notes conclude that "distribution, not willingness to pay, is the bottleneck" and that tools outside Omarchy's menus or AUR-with-votes stay invisible (Screenix, a paid Hyprland-ready recorder, has one AUR vote) ([AUR RPC](https://aur.archlinux.org/rpc/v5/info?arg[]=screenix-bin)). The audience can pay: 1Password's distro telemetry ranks Omarchy #2 on weekdays and #1 on weekends among its Linux users, 826 individuals gave over $120,000 to the Omacom Foundation in roughly three weeks, and the distro ships paid defaults (1Password, Typora, Superwhisper) with a manual page saying "Sometimes the best solution is a commercial offering" ([omarchy.org](https://omarchy.org/news/2026/09/quattro-crosses-200000-iso-downloads); [omarchy.org](https://omarchy.org/news/2026/09/brian-cartmell-and-american-cloud-join-as-open-patronage-doubles/); [Omarchy manual](https://learn.omacom.io/2/the-omarchy-manual/61/commercial)). The Screen Studio clone category is skipped as instructed: OpenScreen (4,065 stars, MIT), Hosaka Studio and Screenix already occupy it, Cap is landing Wayland support, and nothing in the notes justifies a sharply different angle ([OpenScreen](https://github.com/getopenscreen/openscreen); [Cap PR #2420](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/pull/2420)).

## Solo founders spend $115 to $450 a month, almost all of it on six core lines

The 2025 to 2026 "what I pay for" posts converge on the same ten names and the same shape. The most carefully itemised case, at $1k MRR, totals **$148.25/mo**: Vercel Pro $25, Supabase Pro $25, Stripe fees $44, Resend Pro $20, Sentry $8, a domain, and an amortised boilerplate; the same author projects **$400 to $450/mo at $5k MRR**, of which Stripe is about $220 ([Prompts to Product](https://www.promptstoproduct.com/saas-cost-at-1k-mrr)). A second itemised stack lands within 15% of that: $41/mo pre-launch, **$171/mo at ~$2k MRR** (Supabase $25, Stripe $60, Vercel two seats $40, Resend $20, Sentry $26), and $606/mo at $15k MRR where "Stripe fees become the dominant cost" ([socialanimal.dev](https://socialanimal.dev/blog/saas-mvp-cost-2026/)). Founders who refuse managed platforms get far lower: one Rails founder runs everything for about **$45/mo** on Hetzner, DigitalOcean Spaces, Zoho Mail and Postmark ([dev.to](https://dev.to/untaught/how-i-run-my-saas-for-45month-without-supabase-vercel-or-firebase-just-rails-22pc)), and a founder running 14 products solo reports roughly $55 to $95/mo in total ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-real-monthly-cost-of-running-14-saas-products-solo-4611174aad)).

The structural change since 2022 is that AI coding subscriptions ($20 to $40 for Cursor plus Claude or ChatGPT) are now the largest fixed line for pre-revenue founders, displacing the Ahrefs ($99) and Intercom ($89) seats that dominated older lists ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/solopreneur-ai-stack-2026); [dev.to, 2022 stack](https://dev.to/getwaitlist/my-frugal-indie-dev-startup-stack-40b7)). What has not changed is the absence of the second ring. Across roughly ten stack posts, changelog, feedback, status page, link shortener, testimonial, screen recorder and social scheduler tools essentially never appear as paid lines, even though vendors in those categories charge $20 to $90/mo. The stack notes compute that buying one incumbent per secondary category costs **$165 to $300/mo, more than the entire core infra stack**, which is why founders skip them, sit on free tiers or self-build ([Prompts to Product](https://www.promptstoproduct.com/saas-cost-at-1k-mrr); [MicroGaps](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/overpriced-saas-tools-solo-devs-can-replace)). That is the opening: the five shortlisted apps price the whole second ring at $38/mo combined, under a single Canny subscription.

Two caveats on the data. Every total is a single-founder anecdote or a blog estimate; no survey-grade dataset exists, and the blog totals from affiliate-driven sites (saasranger, tools8020) are internally inconsistent. Reddit, Indie Hackers comment threads and X were unreachable to the researchers, so the figures skew toward people who write cost breakdowns on their own blogs, who are likely more frugal than average.

## Resentment follows bills that move on their own, seats, and gated cosmetics

The pain-point research ranked complaint categories by recurrence, and the top of the list is not price level but price motion. Hosting bills that spike without the founder doing anything dominate: a Vercel Pro user's invoice went "from my usual $20-60 to $231.71" after a DDoS, with support calling the charges non-refundable ([Vercel Community](https://community.vercel.com/t/ddos-attack-caused-170-in-extra-charges-support-refuses-refund/44044)); Meta's crawler raised another founder's bill 10x ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49732873)); the MDX.one founder closed signups because they "couldn't afford the bill to keep growing" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/mdx-one/huge-vercel-costs-and-rebrand-to-feather--Myc071NF66jYxLB2yED)). Cursor's June 2025 switch to usage credits produced a public apology and a documented migration to Claude Code and Windsurf ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/07/cursor-apologizes-for-unclear-pricing-changes-that-upset-users/)). Second come short-notice hikes that hit the smallest tier hardest: **Kit raised its 1,000-subscriber Creator plan from $29 to $39, a 34% jump, with about five weeks' notice** ([EmailOctopus](https://emailoctopus.com/blog/kit-formerly-convertkit-price-hike)), Figma restructured seats so design access forces a $20 Full seat ([UserJot](https://userjot.com/blog/figma-pricing-2025-plans-seats-costs-explained)), and Loom converted free viewer seats to paid, so "a workspace with 10 recorders and 90 watchers used to pay for 10 seats. It now pays for 100" ([SaaStr](https://saastr.com/atlassian-just-deleted-looms-free-seats-it-was-also-looms-distribution)). Third is seat pricing as such: "The whole notion of seat-based pricing seems wrong to me" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49876704)). Flat-fee tools like Carrd at $19/yr attract almost no complaints.

Support chat is the category with the most "I built an open-source alternative because of the price" launches: Libredesk's Show HN reached 376 points with commenters calling Zendesk "prohibitive for many small organizations" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43158166)), Opencom launched in February 2026 citing Intercom's "complex and expensive pricing model" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47194407)), and a founder in October 2025 called Intercom "very expensive and constantly upsold, despite being rubbish" while saying Chatwoot "haven't done a great job" as the alternative ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45565815)). That category is not on the shortlist because Chatwoot (37.7k stars, $0 Hacker tier) owns it and the notes found two fresh open-source challengers already fighting for the gap.

The most instructive incident is Cal.com going closed source on 15 April 2026, which generated a **391-point HN thread** of self-hosters saying "Time to migrate" and accusing the company of "using opensource as GTM strategy, to get developer contribution, traction and then say fuckoff" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47780456); [WhyOps](https://whyops.com/blog/cal-com-closed-source)). Screen Studio's removal of its lifetime licence in October 2025 drew the same reaction and sent users to Cap and Screenity ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/10/screen-studio-has-removed-their-non-subscription-licensing-options-for-new-customers/)). The lesson for the shortlist is that the self-host promise is itself the product, and it has to be structurally credible: AGPL, no CLA that permits relicensing, no feature gating, and a dependency footprint a founder can actually run. The gated features that matter to a solo founder are cosmetic and ownership features (custom domain, branding removal, export), which is why every paid tier above puts exactly those behind a flat fee and nothing else.

What stops founders from self-hosting the current options is time and ops weight, not ideology. "If I spend 15 mins of time every month extra self hosting then it's immediately not worth it" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46850994)); "people just say fuck it and pay someone else" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45538496)). The landscape audit of docker-compose files shows why: Rybbit, Plausible, OpenPanel and Langfuse need ClickHouse; Twenty, Cal.com, Docmost, OpnForm and Lago need Postgres plus Redis; Postiz's dev compose pulls Postgres, Redis, Temporal and Elasticsearch ([Rybbit compose](https://github.com/rybbit-io/rybbit/blob/master/docker-compose.yml); [Postiz compose](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app/blob/main/docker-compose.dev.yaml)). Only Listmonk, Uptime Kuma, Gatus, Umami, Documenso, Logto and Fider are solo-founder-friendly to run. The landscape notes conclude that a Cloudflare-native Workers plus D1/KV/R2 design "would differentiate in status pages, waitlists, testimonials, short links and feedback boards, since none of the leaders there run without Postgres," which is precisely the list above. One category is deliberately excluded for the same reason: outbound email. Listmonk has 23.8k stars and no cloud, and Keila's EUR 8/mo cloud is the only indie-priced option ([listmonk.app](https://listmonk.app/); [Keila](https://www.keila.io/pricing)), but HN self-hosting enthusiasts are near-unanimous that "self-hosting your own outbound email is not worth it" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49966767)), deliverability is a full-time job, and a hosted newsletter would compete on SPF/DKIM reputation rather than product.

## The Postiz playbook: stars buy distribution, a workflow buys revenue

Postiz's arc is the template and the warning. Nevo David repositioned an existing repo as "Postiz" around September 2024, posted eight Show HNs in four months, won Product Hunt on 20 November 2024 with 898 votes, hit #1 on GitHub Trending three days later, and reached roughly **14k stars in three months** ([dev.to](https://dev.to/nevodavid/i-built-an-open-source-social-media-scheduling-and-it-blew-up-415e); [aitoolly](https://aitoolly.com/nl/producthunt-daily/2024-11-20); [Star History](https://www.star-history.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app)). Revenue did not follow stars. For about eighteen months, 25k stars translated to under $20k MRR, under one dollar of MRR per star, and the founder himself describes the period as flat "about two years" ([Munich Startup](https://insights.munich-startup.de/news/feed/open-source-saas-founder-grows-postiz-to-17k-month-with-25k-github-stars); [postiz.com blog](https://postiz.com/blog/saas-marketing-strategy-chatgpt-astra-2-2m-arr)). The step change to $118k MRR by June 2026 and $260k by October came when he shipped an MCP server, a CLI and an agent skill, and a user's post about Postiz plus OpenClaw got 7.2M views and roughly 700 trials a day: "My revenue just exploded once I started sending to agents" ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/revenue-jumped-when-he-sold-to-ai-agents/); [Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/17k-to-143k-mrr-the-agentic-pivot); [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/postiz)). Churn fell from about 28% to 13.7% because automated posting is a recurring workflow, and that surge was partly bought (influencer reposts at $60 to $350 each, UGC creators at $20 to $45 a video), not purely organic.

The pricing mechanics are the part to copy exactly. Postiz does not gate: "We do not 'gate' features or limit the license," every self-hosted install "behaves as the top tier, with no limits," and the cloud sells convenience, pre-registered social platform developer apps, metered AI credits, hosting and priority support at **$29 to $99/mo with a 7-day trial and no free cloud plan** ([Postiz README](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app); [Postiz cloud docs](https://docs.postiz.com/cloud/overview); [postiz.com/pricing](https://postiz.com/pricing)). The license moved from Apache-2.0 to AGPL-3.0 on 6 January 2025, one month after the launch spike, as the standard anti-rehosting defence; there is no CLA, which locks the project into AGPL or more permissive and is itself a trust signal after Cal.com. For the shortlist this means: AGPL from day one, no CLA, self-host as the top tier, and a hosted tier that charges for the thing a solo founder cannot or will not run (custom domains, email sending through Resend, R2 video storage, multi-region checks, Stripe plumbing).

The comparables confirm the timeline and the shape. Papermark went from a tweet to roughly **$20k MRR in year one and $75k MRR mid-year two** with ~1,000 paying out of ~30,000 users, bootstrapped, and its founders say self-hosters convert "because we do the best service in hosting the solution" ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/how-we-built-it-900k-open-source-saas)). Rybbit, the closest analogue in scale to what one person can build, launched in April 2025, reached 13k stars in about a year and sits at **$9,215 MRR on 318 subscriptions at $13 to $26/mo** ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/rybbit-analytics); [Gappsy](https://gappsy.com/tools/rybbit/)). The warnings are equally consistent: Cloudron moved to drop the Postiz package in February 2026 after a new Temporal dependency made it "pretty clear that upstream is not really intending this to be a production ready selfhosted app" ([Cloudron forum](https://forum.cloudron.io/topic/15047/plan-to-drop-postiz-package-from-cloudron)); the maintainer admits "documentation is lacking" and that providers are hard to add ([Lemmy via scribe](https://scribe.disroot.org/post/1945251)); and a second-hand report puts support at five to ten tickets a day, mostly from Portainer, Coolify and Unraid users. A Cloudflare-only architecture sidesteps most of that ticket volume because there is one deploy path and no Docker, but it also means the self-host story is "bring your own Cloudflare account," which should be stated plainly in each README. Expect twelve to twenty-four months under $20k MRR even on a top-1% star curve, and plan the five builds so that the cheap public-facing ones (status page, testimonial walls, short links) generate backlinks for the two higher-ticket ones.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "which SaaS can be cloned" to "which categories combine a price-expectation gap, a thin open-source field, and a product shape that runs on Workers and D1 without a Postgres cluster." Five categories pass all three tests, and they are not the ones founders complain about loudest (Vercel, Cursor, Intercom), because those are either infrastructure that Cloudflare already solves or categories where Chatwoot-class projects exist. They are the quiet second ring that founders refuse to pay $29 to $90 for and have no credible way to self-host today. Pricing the whole ring at $38/mo flat, with the self-host as the top tier, is a deliberate inversion of the incumbents' per-seat and percentage-take models and of the current open-source leaders' Docker-heavy footprints.

The Postiz evidence sets expectations: a strong launch can produce five-figure stars in a quarter, revenue lags by a year or more, and the inflection comes from a recurring workflow and an agent or CLI surface, not from more stars. That argues for shipping the feedback board first (it reuses the upvote and newsletter Workers, so it is the fastest path to a public repo), giving every app a public API and a CLI from the start so agents and Omarchy launchers can drive them, and treating the Omarchy audience, which pays for software at unusual rates and whose distro already endorses commercial tools, as the first thousand users rather than a side market.
